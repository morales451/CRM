1. Getting started

  • Start the app: double-click start.bat (Windows) or run ./start.sh (Mac/Linux). First run installs everything; after that it starts in seconds.
  • On this computer: open http://localhost:8000.
  • On your phone/tablet: the startup window prints an address like http://192.168.1.23:8000 — type it into your phone's browser (must be on the same Wi-Fi). Then use Add to Home Screen so it opens like an app with its own icon.
  • One-time setup: on the Templates page, check "Your Info" (name, title, company, phone, email, address) — it fills into every script and invoice. On the Import page, set the off-machine backup folder.
  • Keep the computer awake while you work from your phone — if it sleeps, the app is unreachable until it wakes.

2. The daily routine

  1. Open the Dashboard. The top row tells you today's workload at a glance.
  2. Hit ▶ Work the Queue and go task by task: script on screen, call/email/text with one tap, log it, next.
  3. Chase money: any invoice in the 💰 card is waiting on payment — overdue ones are red.
  4. Glance at 🥶 Going Stale and give each one a follow-up date so it re-enters the flow.
Everything in this CRM flows from one habit: log every touch. Logged interactions clear reminders, feed the timeline, drive Insights, and keep deals from going stale.

3. Dashboard

  • Today's progress bar sits at the top — "16 of 24 done today", counting everything you logged or checked off today against what's still waiting. It turns green and says the list is clear when nothing's left.
  • It stays fast on a big list. The dashboard draws the top 25 due reminders rather than all of them — the count and the Queue button always show the real total, and Show all draws the lot if you want it. Same idea on the Accounts page (100 per page) and on an account's history (25 entries, with show all). This is what keeps a click instant once you're past a few hundred accounts.
  • 🎯 / 📅 toggle (top right) — choose whether today's work is ordered by matching buildings or by due date. See Priority.
  • 📌 Due Reminders — cold-cadence steps due today (or overdue), with each account's 🎯 matching-building count. Each row: 📄 Script opens the personalized script, ✓ Log records the step (clears the reminder), ✕ Skip clears without logging. A reminder stays pinned until you do one of those.
  • 💰 Outstanding Invoices — every sent, unpaid invoice; overdue first in red; ✓ Mark Paid on the spot.
  • 📅 Follow-Ups Due — accounts whose follow-up date has arrived, with the note you left yourself, snooze buttons (+1d/+3d/+1w) and ✓ Done.
  • 🥶 Going Stale — pipeline deals with no logged touch in 14+ days and no follow-up scheduled. One tap sets "follow up today."
  • 🎯 Top Priority Accounts — your highest-potential live accounts by matching buildings.
  • 🕘 Recent Activity — your last 10 logged interactions.

4. Work the Queue

One task at a time, combining cadence steps and follow-ups in your chosen order (🎯 Priority by default — biggest matching portfolios first; see Priority). For a cadence task you get the account's info, contact buttons, and every matching script (call script + voicemail + text together for call days) with Copy buttons and one-tap Open in Email app / Dial / Open in Messages. Then:

  • ✓ Log … & Next — records the step and advances.
  • ✕ Skip this step — clears the step without logging it.
  • Skip for now → — leaves the task in place and shows the next one; ← Back steps back.

Call steps get outcome buttons, right under the account so you don't scroll past the scripts after every call: No answer, Voicemail, Gatekeeper, Spoke, Meeting booked, Not interested, Bad number — numbered 1–7. One tap logs the call and moves on. See call outcomes.

Keyboard shortcuts for calling blocks: L log & next, S skip the step, N or → next task, P or ← previous, 1–7 call outcome, E exit, ? for the list. They're ignored while you're typing in a box.

Follow-up tasks show your note, a quick log form, snooze buttons, and ✓ Done.

4b. Call outcomes

  • Logging a call — from the queue, the dashboard's ✓ Log call menu, or the account page — records how it went: No answer, Voicemail, Gatekeeper, Spoke, Meeting booked, Not interested, Bad number.
  • Three of them also move the account for you:
    • Meeting booked → Pipeline Milestone becomes Accepted Meeting, which ends the cadence.
    • Not interested → Prospecting Status becomes Not Interested, which ends the cadence.
    • Bad number → the account goes back to Research with a dated note. That call is not counted as done, so once you fix the number (or add another contact) the cadence picks up again with the call still to make.
  • Every one-tap log is undoable — the log and whatever it changed. A mis-tap on "Not interested" mid-call-block is one click to take back.
  • Outcomes show as badges on the account timeline and Recent Activity, can be corrected afterwards from the timeline's ✎ editor, and go into the Excel export.

5. Importing lists (CoStar etc.)

  • Upload .xlsx or .csv on the Import page. Columns are matched by meaning, not name or position — "Company", "Account Name", "Cell #", "# Properties (in search)", "Office Phone Number" all work. The summary shows exactly how each column mapped; ignored columns are listed.
  • Pacing: set "Start N /business day" — about a fifth of the touches you can do in a day — each account brings five touches over ten business days, so at 10 a day you settle at exactly 50 tasks a day — so a big list becomes a steady daily routine instead of hundreds of Day-1 tasks. Forgot? Use Re-Pace Cadence — it re-staggers all untouched in-cadence accounts and never moves anyone you've already contacted.
  • Safe re-uploads: duplicate companies are skipped, missing property counts are backfilled onto existing accounts, and anything you archived stays out.
  • Imported accounts capture address/city/zip into Notes, the website into its own field (contact uploads match on it), the criteria-matching property count (🎯), and total properties owned. Rows with a named person plus an email or phone go straight into the cadence; the rest wait in Research until you add someone.

6. Priority: biggest portfolios first

Accounts carry two property counts from your CoStar import: 🎯 matching (buildings that fit your criteria — the "# Properties (in search)" column) and total owned. The matching count is the money number, so the CRM sorts your work by it.

  • Dashboard toggle: 🎯 Priority (default) works the accounts with the most matching buildings first; 📅 Due date is the classic oldest-first order. Your choice is saved and also drives Work the Queue, so the biggest opportunities get your freshest hours.
  • 🎯 column on every due reminder, follow-up and stale row, plus a Buildings in today's tasks tile totalling the opportunity sitting in your queue right now.
  • 🎯 Top Priority Accounts card lists your eight highest-potential live accounts (matching count, total owned, status, last touch) — the shortlist worth extra effort.
  • Accounts page: sort by 🎯 Most buildings (default), and filter with 🎯 ≥ N to see only accounts above a threshold — e.g. type 10 to work only double-digit portfolios. The header badge totals the matching buildings in the current view.
  • Scripts already cite this number: {matching_properties} is what Email 1 means by "roughly N properties built before the 1980s."
A prospect with 40 matching buildings is worth many calls; one with a single building is worth one email. Priority order makes that automatic instead of something you have to remember.

6b. Archiving: removing accounts for good

When a company isn't worth your time — bad data, wrong company, do-not-contact, already has a contractor — archive it instead of deleting it. On the account page open 🗄 Archive / Remove, pick a reason, and it's gone from your working life:

  • Hidden from the dashboard, queue, accounts list, pipeline, insights and recent activity.
  • Its cadence stops and any follow-up is cleared.
  • Future imports skip it. Re-upload the same CoStar list and it will not come back; the import summary tells you how many rows were skipped because you archived them.
  • Nothing is lost — interactions, bids and projects stay attached. Find it under Accounts → 🗄 Archived and hit ↩ Restore to put it back on the list.
Archive vs delete: archiving is the one you want almost always — it remembers the company so it can't sneak back in on the next import. 🗑 Delete permanently erases the account and its history completely, which also means the CRM forgets it ever existed, so a future import can add that company again.

6c. Bulk actions & search

  • Tick and apply. Every row on the Accounts page has a checkbox, and the header box selects the whole filtered list. Once anything is ticked an action bar appears: set Prospecting Status, set Pipeline Milestone, set or clear a follow-up date, restart the cadence from today, or archive — all in one click. On the Archived view the actions become Restore and Delete permanently — and only archived accounts can be deleted, so a live account can't be erased by mistake.
  • Every bulk action is undoable — see section 14b.
  • Search looks everywhere. The search box matches the company name, the primary contact, every other contact on the account, job titles, management level, the notes, and phone numbers typed in any format (search 5557777 and it finds “(713) 555-7777”). When a row matched because of someone in its contact list, the list says which person.

7. The cold cadence

  • Counted in business days. Day 1 is the day the cadence starts; every later step counts working days, so nothing lands on a weekend and Monday doesn't inherit the weekend's work. Started on a Friday: Email 1 Friday, the call Tuesday, Call 2 Friday, Email 2 Tuesday, the breakup Thursday.
  • The clock resets every time you act. Each step is due a set number of business days after the step before it: the call 2 days after Email 1, Call 2 3 days after the call, Email 2 2 days after that, and the breakup 2 days after Email 2. The count runs from the day you actually did the step (or skipped it). Send Email 1 a week late and the call is due two business days after you sent it, not overdue the moment you log the email. If a step isn't done, the steps after it keep counting from its due date, so an untouched account still shows as behind.
  • 🔎 Research: no clock until there's someone to contact. An account only starts its cadence once it has a named person with an email or phone — the first step is an email, and a switchboard number can't receive one. Until then it waits on the Research list with no overdue tasks, biggest portfolios first: that list is who to look up in ZoomInfo next. The moment you add a contact — typing them in, pasting a ZoomInfo page or roster, or uploading a ZoomInfo export — the cadence starts that day. Start the cadence anyway on the account page is there for calling a main line before you have a name.
  • When this version first runs it moves untouched accounts with nobody to contact into Research and prints how many in the startup window. Anything you've already worked keeps its dates.
  • You see one step per account, not all of them. The cadence is a sequence — you can't send Email 1 and the Breakup Email to the same person on the same day — so an account shows up once, at the step it's waiting on. Log or skip it and it leaves today's list until its next step comes due. If it's fallen behind, the row says "+N more steps behind".
  • If everything is due at once, it's because a big import put every account on the same clock. Re-Pace Cadence on the Import page re-staggers the untouched ones — pick about a fifth of the touches you can do in a day — each account brings five touches over ten business days, so at 10 a day you settle at exactly 50 tasks a day — and never moves an account you've already contacted.

8. Contacts & ZoomInfo

The main way: a whole batch of companies at once

Don't open companies one by one. Send ZoomInfo the whole Research list, export the people you want from every one of those companies in one go, and upload that file. Fifty companies take about the same time as one.

  1. CRM → ZoomInfo. Open Accounts → 🔎 Research and press ⬇ Download for ZoomInfo. You get a CSV of those companies with their website and address, biggest portfolios first. A search or 🎯 filter on the page narrows the file the same way, so 🎯 ≥ 5 gives you just the big owners.
  2. In ZoomInfo: upload that CSV as a company list (Lists → import/upload). ZoomInfo matches each row to its company, using the website when it has one.
  3. Go to Advanced Search → Contacts, filter to that company list, and add your ICP filters: Management Level (C-Level, VP-Level, Director), and Department or Job Function if you want facilities, asset or property people.
  4. Look over the results, untick anyone you don't want, and Export. Each exported contact can use a ZoomInfo credit, so the filters are also what keeps the cost down. The stock export (CSV or Excel) works as it is, so you don't need to edit it.
  5. ZoomInfo → CRM. On the Import page, choose the file under Import Contacts and press Upload. Put a number in Start N /business day if the batch is big. With 60 companies and a 10 there, the cadences start 10 per working day rather than all at once.

What happens on upload:

  • Each person attaches to their company by name. If the name doesn't match, they attach by web domain: the export's Email Domain/Website against the account's website. So “Harlow Enterprises” still finds your “HARLOW ENT HOLDINGS LTD” if that account's website is harlowenterprises.com. Every domain match is listed in the summary so you can check it. A domain shared by two of your accounts doesn't match either one, and free mail domains (gmail, yahoo …) never count. The emails of people already on an account aren't used, because a property manager's management firm would otherwise drag that firm's own staff onto the owner's account. An account gets a website from your CoStar list, from you, or from the first export that matches it by name.
  • The first person on an account becomes its primary and the rest go under Contacts. Change the primary any time with ★ on the account page.
  • Direct lines with an extension, like ZoomInfo's (713) 555-0100 ext. 32, dial correctly: 📞 dials the number, pauses, then keys the extension. If someone has no direct line, the company's HQ phone from the export is used.
  • The account's cadence starts (it leaves Research), people already on the account are skipped, archived companies are left alone, and the website is saved onto the account if it didn't have one.
  • The whole upload can be undone from the Undo bar. That removes the people it added, puts the accounts back exactly as they were, and returns them to Research.

One company at a time

  • Exported the people for one company? On that account, use 📥 Upload a ZoomInfo export for this company in the Contacts card, choose the file, and press Add everyone in the file. There's no copying and pasting, and the company name in the file doesn't have to match. People already on the account are skipped, and it can be undone.
  • 🔎 ZoomInfo opens the company's own ZoomInfo page once the CRM knows it. It learns the page from a ZoomInfo upload (the export's company columns), from pasting a ZoomInfo page, or from you pasting the address into ZoomInfo company page under Account Info. Re-uploading an old export fills it in for accounts you already have. Until then, the button searches for the company. It opens signed in as long as you're signed in to ZoomInfo in that browser.
  • Each account has a primary contact (fills scripts, emails, invoices) plus unlimited additional people.
  • On an account page: 🔎 ZoomInfo / in LinkedIn buttons jump to research for that company; the + Add Contact form is right below. The first person you add becomes primary automatically; ★ promotes anyone else to primary (the old primary moves down, nothing is lost).
  • Paste a whole ZoomInfo page instead of typing anything. On the contact's page press Ctrl+A then Ctrl+C. In the CRM open + Add Contact, paste into the box and press 📋 Read the paste. The boxes below fill in so you can check them before pressing Add Contact.
    • No careful selecting needed — menus, buttons and the colleagues listed further down the page (Similar Contacts, Org Chart, People Also Viewed) are thrown away.
    • It finds the contact by anchoring on the company link in the page header — ZoomInfo stacks the header as name, job title, company, so the two lines above that link are the person. Where there is an email, the surname in it confirms the choice. Either way the people listed further down the page can't get mixed in.
    • Pages copied from a browser arrive as Markdown, so links look like [Harlow Enterprises](https://…). Those are unpacked, and a link to somebody else's profile is skipped.
    • Not every contact has a revealed email. The phones, name, title and company still come through, and no email is invented.
    • The Contact Details panel's headings are ignored and its type tags are read: (B) business, (HQ) head office, (D) direct, (M) mobile. That's what decides which number is the mobile and which is the work line — and a direct dial beats the HQ switchboard.
    • Copying only the Contact Details panel gives you the email and phones but no name, because ZoomInfo keeps the name at the top of the page. Grab the whole page instead, or type the name first — anything you've typed is kept when you press Read the paste.
    • If there's still no name and the email is in first.last@ form, the name is taken from the address and flagged so you check the spelling. A run-together address like mdelacruz@ is left alone rather than guessed at.
    • A "Label: value" list and a row copied out of a spreadsheet work too. A paste with no contact in it is refused rather than half-filled.
  • Several people, one paste — the fastest way in. On the company's Employees tab in ZoomInfo:
    1. Narrow the list with the management-level chips at the top (C-Level, VP-Level, Director) so you're looking at your targets.
    2. Expand the three or four rows you actually want — the ▾ on each person's row, which reveals their email and phone numbers inline. This is the step that matters: a collapsed row has nothing to give.
    3. Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C once.
    4. In the CRM: + Add Contact → Or paste a company employee list → 👥 Find people.
    Everyone on the page is listed with their title, management level and — for the rows you expanded — their email, work phone and mobile. Nothing is ticked to start with. Tick your three or four, optionally mark one as primary, and press Add Selected. They're created complete, in one go, without opening a single profile.
    • Tick decision-makers selects every C-Level, VP-Level and Director at once if you'd rather deselect than select.
    • A row you didn't expand shows "— row not expanded —" and is still worth adding for the name and title; you can fill in the rest later.
    • People already on the account are greyed out, so you can paste the same page next month and only see who's new.
    • Where a person has both a direct dial and the company switchboard, the direct dial becomes their work phone.
  • How names match on upload: legal suffixes (LLC, Inc, LP, Corp, P.C.) and punctuation are ignored, so “Hartman Income REIT, Inc.” finds your “Hartman Income REIT LP”. Words like Partners, Holdings and Group count as part of the name, so “ABC Partners” and “ABC Holdings” stay separate. When a primary's direct dial replaces the company main line, the main line is kept in Notes.
  • Companies you don't have yet: tick “Open a new account for any company I don't have yet” on the upload and those rows become new prospecting accounts instead of being listed back as unmatched. Archived companies are never re-created.
  • Columns read automatically from a stock ZoomInfo export: Company Name, First/Last Name, Job Title, Email Address, Direct Phone Number, Mobile phone, LinkedIn Contact Profile URL, Management Level, Website, Email Domain and Company HQ Phone. The rest of ZoomInfo's 70-odd columns are ignored. The import summary lists exactly which column it read for each field.
  • LinkedIn URLs become a clickable link on the contact; Management Level shows as a badge (C-Level, VP-Level, Director, Manager) and is guessed from the job title when the file doesn't have it. Both are searchable and both go into the Excel export.

8b. Calls, texts & email: which app opens

The CRM never sends anything itself. Its buttons open your own phone or mail app with everything filled in, so calls come from your number and emails come from your inbox. Choose the apps under Templates → Your Info.

  • 📞 / 💬 → Phone's own dialer (the default): rings straight from the phone you're holding, and "ext. 32" is dialed for you.
  • 📞 / 💬 → Google Voice: opens voice.google.com ready to call or text the number, so the other person sees your Google Voice number, not your cell. It works on the computer and in the phone's browser. Google Voice can't dial an extension for you, so key it in when the call connects. A text opens with no message written, so copy the script with its Copy button first. Android shortcut: in the Google Voice app's settings, set calls to go through Google Voice. Then you can leave the CRM on "Phone's own dialer", and every 📞 still goes out on your Google Voice number, extension included.
  • ✉️ → Gmail or Outlook in the browser: opens a new message with the subject and body already written, in a new tab. On an iPhone it opens the Gmail or Outlook app's new-message screen instead. Nothing is sent until you press Send. "Default mail app" uses whatever your computer or phone opens for email links.
  • Two Gmail accounts? Put the CRM one in Send CRM email from. On the computer, Gmail then always opens that account's compose window, even with your personal Gmail signed in. On an iPhone the Gmail app can't be told which account to use. Instead, add only the CRM account to Apple's Mail app (Settings → Apps → Mail → Mail Accounts), make Mail the default mail app (Settings → Apps → Mail → Default Mail App), and set On a phone, Email buttons open to "Phone's default mail app". Keep reading personal mail in the Gmail app.
  • Signature: set it once under Templates → Your Info → Email signature, and it's added to the end of every email template. It replaces a template's closing name lines, so your name appears only once. On an iPhone, clear Mail's own signature (Settings → Apps → Mail → Signature) so "Sent from my iPhone" isn't added after it.
  • No ✉️ button? The account has no email on file. The page now says No email on file, and the scripts page says what to add. Fill it in under Account Info, or upload the person from ZoomInfo.
  • Set Your phone on the same page to your Google Voice number, because it's the number the scripts and voicemail tell people to call back.

8c. Door knocking

Door knocks sit alongside the cadence and never change its steps or dates. They answer one question per company: have I been through that door?

  • Every account has an office address (Account Info → Office address). It comes from your CoStar import, from a ZoomInfo contact upload (the company's street, city, state and zip), or from you. Addresses older imports put in Notes were moved into these fields automatically.
  • The account page shows 🚪 Never visited or 🚪 Visited with the date and how it went, plus 📍 Map for directions.
  • 🚪 Door knock logs a visit in two taps: Met decision-maker, Left info, Gatekeeper, Closed / no access, Wrong address, Meeting booked or Not interested, with an optional note. Meeting booked and Not interested move the deal exactly as they do on a call. Visits appear in the history like everything else.
  • Planning a day out: on Accounts, set the 🚪 filter to Never visited (add 🎯 ≥ 5 for the big owners, or search a zip such as 77006), then press ⬇ Door-knock list. You get a spreadsheet of those offices sorted by zip, so neighbouring offices sit together, with the contact, phone and building count for each.

9. Scripts & templates

  • Your cold call script, voicemail, text, Email 1, Email 2, and breakup email live on the Templates page — fully editable, with checkboxes controlling which cadence steps each appears for.
  • Placeholders fill from each account when a script opens: {first_name} {last_name} {title} {company} {matching_properties} (fit your pre-1980s criteria) {num_properties} (total) {my_name} {my_company} {my_phone}.
  • Missing info renders as a bracketed hint like [first name] with a warning — nothing half-personalized slips out unnoticed.
  • Open in Email app pre-fills recipient, subject and body — you just press Send. Calls dial; texts open Messages pre-typed.

10. Follow-ups & stale deals

  • Set a follow-up date + note on any account (quick buttons: Tomorrow / +3 days / +1 week). Due follow-ups pin to the Dashboard and Queue until done or snoozed — this is how warm prospects outside the cold cadence stay visible.
  • Pipeline deals with no touch in 14+ days and no follow-up set get flagged 🥶 on the Dashboard.

11. Pipeline board

A column per milestone (Accepted Meeting → … → Closed Won/Lost), each deal as a card showing the contact, last touch, and any follow-up date. The dropdown on each card moves the deal to another milestone right from the board.

12. Roof reports / bids

  • On an account page, 📑 Roof Reports / Bids → + New Report (the address auto-fills from the imported notes).
  • Fill in roof size, non-coated deductions, surface type, warranty years, price, assessment date, and observations (one per line — they become the report's bullet list).
  • Photos: upload straight from your phone — auto-resized, auto-rotated, converted to compact JPEGs. Captions print beside each photo in the two-per-row survey grid.
  • 📑 Open Report generates the full branded proposal in plain English, matching your current template: cover, CEO letter personalized to the contact, the four-step project journey plus the three-phase on-roof work table, roof facts, site assessment + photo survey, a Your Project At A Glance summary box, the coating steps with a "why it matters" line on each, a materials quote computed from the square footage (1.25 gal/sq basecoat, 2 gal/sq topcoat, +5% waste, rounded to 5-gal pails), Your Investment in words and figures, both warranties plus Material Plus, next steps, and the acceptance/signature page. Print / Save as PDF from the browser.
  • Products: pick the topcoat, basecoat and butter grade per bid — Henry Prograde names are the defaults, Enduraroof equivalents are the alternates, and the list only ever shows products that belong to the chosen system. The proposal names the exact products, and the warranty holder and primer names follow the brand you picked (Prograde → Henry Company; Enduraroof → Enduraroof).
  • Only real combinations: the system, roof category and warranty dropdowns filter each other, so you can't build a job that doesn't exist (aluminum is 10-year only, on capsheet or metal; reinforced acrylic isn't offered over metal or sprayfoam). If a saved bid somehow holds an impossible combination it's snapped to the nearest real one with a notice, and a report with no computable spec prints a red warning instead of inventing one.
  • 🧮 Warranty Calculator (built in): pick the coating system (Silicone / Acrylic / Aluminum), the roof category (Capsheet / Single-Ply / Sprayfoam / Metal) and the warranty length, and the report quotes the manufacturer's real application rates for that exact combination — e.g. silicone on capsheet is 2 gal/square of topcoat for 10 years but 2.5 for 15 and 3 for 20. Add linear feet for butter-grade mastic, waste/stretch %, and flag a failed adhesion test or rust to add primer. The editor shows the full materials plan (rates, gallons, pails) plus a 10/15/20-year comparison so you can price options.
  • 💵 Suggested price: the editor prices the job from your per-sq-ft rules — capsheet $4.50/sq ft for a 10-year system, $4.65 at 15 and $4.75 at 20; every other surface $4.00 / $4.15 / $4.25 — multiplied by the coated (net) area. Use this price drops it into the quote, and if your quote differs you'll see by what percentage. Edit the rates any time under 💵 Pricing on the Templates page.
  • Warranty length flows through the whole document — set it to 10 and every mention (glance box, top coat spec, warranty section) says 10 years.

13. Projects & invoicing (after Closed Won)

  • Move a deal to Closed Won and its account page offers Create Project (the Projects page also lists won deals missing one).
  • Every project starts with the standard job checklist — contract → deposit → materials → crew → job → walkthrough → final payment → warranty. Tap to check off (timestamped), add or remove steps, watch the progress bar.
  • Invoices: add as Draft → Mark Sent (stamps the date; due date defaults to +30 days) → ✓ Paid. Sent invoices pin to the Dashboard until paid; past-due ones show Overdue.
  • 🖨 on any invoice row prints a branded invoice — logo, your info, bill-to with the customer's address, PAID/OVERDUE stamps, and your payment terms (late fee, card fee, lien waivers) in the footer. Print or save as PDF to email.
  • Money rolls up everywhere: per project, across the Projects page, and on Insights (contracted / invoiced / collected / outstanding).

14. Insights

  • Tiles: tasks due, touches this week (with vs-last-week arrow — your effort meter), active prospects, in-cadence pool, closed won, win rate — plus a money row once projects exist.
  • Daily activity: interactions logged per day for the last 14 days against your daily goal (set it on the Templates page; 0 hides the goal line). Bars that reach the goal turn green, weekends are faded, and the heading says how many weekdays you hit and your weekday average. This is the chart that tells you whether a slow month is an activity problem or a conversion problem — while there's still time to fix it.
  • Calls — last 30 days: calls made, how many reached the person, your connect rate, and meetings booked as a share of connects, with a bar per outcome. Low connect rate means a list or timing problem; low meetings per connect means a pitch problem. Rates only count calls logged with an outcome.
  • Weekly activity: interactions logged per week, last 8 weeks — the longer trend behind the daily chart.
  • Pipeline: where deals pile up. Cadence reach: how many accounts got each step — a steep drop from emails to calls means calls are being skipped. Status breakdown and activity mix round it out.

14b. Undo & fixing mistakes

  • Undo bar. Delete an account, contact, roof report, photo, project, task or invoice — or apply any bulk action — and a bar appears at the top of the page saying what just happened, with an ↶ Undo button. Undo puts everything back: the account and its contacts, history, bids, projects, invoices and photo files. The bar shows for 15 minutes after the action, and each record can only be replayed once.
  • Deleted bid photos wait in a trash folder for 7 days so an undo can restore the images themselves, not just their database rows. After that they're cleared automatically on startup.
  • Fixing a logged interaction. On the account timeline, the ✎ next to any entry opens an editor: change the type, the notes, or the date, or delete the entry. The date matters — reminders are worked out from what's logged, so correcting the date corrects the cadence. Deleting a logged cadence step brings its reminder back.
  • Duplicate accounts. Imports now treat “Boxer Property Corp” and “Boxer Property, Corp.” as the same company, so a re-pull from CoStar can't create a second account or un-archive one you removed. Whenever an import row is matched to an account spelled differently, the summary lists the pair so you can check it. Duplicates created before that are listed at the bottom of the Import page. Pick the one to keep — the one that's been worked most is pre-selected — and press Merge: people, history, roof reports, projects and checked-off steps all move onto it, gaps in its details are filled from the others, their notes are appended (labelled), and the duplicates are removed. Undo puts every piece back, photos and invoices included. An archived duplicate never blocks imports for the live company.

15. Data safety

  • Your whole CRM is one file, crm.db. Bid photos live in the uploads/ folder next to it.
  • A dated backup is saved to backups/ automatically once a day (even if the app runs for weeks), newest 14 kept.
  • Off-machine: set the backup folder on the Import page to a synced folder (OneDrive/Google Drive/Dropbox) and every daily backup is mirrored there. Download Full Backup grabs a snapshot from any device.
  • Restore: stop the app, copy a backup file over crm.db, start again.
  • 📊 Export Everything (Excel) on the Import or Insights page builds one workbook with a summary tab plus accounts, contacts, full interaction history, roof reports (including computed rates and suggested prices), projects, invoices and today's tasks — every sheet frozen and filterable. The birds-eye view of the whole business.
  • CSV exports of accounts and interaction history are on the Import page.

15b. Updating the app without losing data

Your data no longer lives in the app folder. It sits in Documents\RoofCRM (the startup window prints the exact path), which means you can replace, delete or re-download the app folder as often as you like and your accounts, photos and backups stay put. The first time you run this version, anything left in the old location is moved there automatically and the startup window lists what moved.

It updates itself

Every time you start the app with start.bat, it checks for a newer version and installs it first. That takes a second or two. There's nothing to download or unzip, and you don't need Git. The startup window says Already up to date or Updated N file(s). Offline? It says so and starts the version you have.

  • App already open and you want the latest now? Close its window and double-click start.bat again. update.bat also updates without starting.
  • Only the app's own files change. Your data in Documents\RoofCRM is never touched, and if a download fails partway through, nothing is changed.
  • New requirements are installed automatically when a version needs them.
  • To turn off the check on start, set an environment variable ROOF_CRM_NO_AUTO_UPDATE=1. update.bat still works.
  • A folder made with git clone updates with git pull instead, so its history stays intact.

One-time catch: a copy older than this feature doesn't have the updater yet, so it needs one last zip download. After that you won't need a zip again.

Belt and braces: before any update, hit ⬇ Download Full Backup on the Import page, or just confirm your off-machine backup folder is set. Takes five seconds and makes an update completely reversible.

Want your data somewhere else (a synced drive, say)? Set an environment variable ROOF_CRM_DATA to that folder and the app will use it.

16. Troubleshooting

  • A page shows an error. It names the error and has a Copy details button — that text says which line failed. The same thing is written to error.log next to your database (the startup window prints the path). Everything else still works and your data is untouched.
  • Phone can't reach the app: same Wi-Fi network? Computer awake? Windows Firewall allowed on private networks? (Re-run the app and accept the firewall prompt, or allow inbound port 8000.)
  • iPhone photos won't upload: they're likely HEIC. Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible, or share as JPEG.
  • Import mapped a column wrong: the import summary shows every mapping — rename the header in Excel to something plainer (e.g. "Company Name") and re-upload; duplicates are skipped so it's safe.
  • Wrong "Your Info" on scripts/invoices: fix it once on the Templates page.
  • Health check: python3 tests/test_crm.py runs the app's 178-check test suite against a throwaway database.
